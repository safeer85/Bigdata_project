Each rule carries a \code{for:} duration of 1--2 real minutes. The
durations are short on purpose: at \code{COMPRESSION=60}, one real
minute is one simulated hour, so a conventional five-minute
\code{for:} would mean waiting five simulated hours before an alert
fired. Every rule has a corresponding entry in
\code{docs/runbook.md} giving what it means, how to reproduce it and how
to fix it.

by \textbf{pipeline stage}, so that each member owned one end-to-end
vertical slice --- its source code, its schema objects, its tests and its
sections of this report --- rather than all three editing the same files.

\section*{Decisions each member defends}

\begin{description}
  \item[Ahamed N.F.F.] Partitioning by \code{vehicle\_id} as an
        ordering requirement (Section~\ref{sec:impl-partitioning}); the
        60$\times$ simulated clock and its derivation from a persisted
        start instant (Section~\ref{sec:simclock}); the deliberate fault
        rates (Section~\ref{sec:impl-faults}); and the decision that
        validation rejects rather than repairs.
  \item[Safeer S.M.] The deliberately boring archiver
        (Section~\ref{sec:impl-archiver}); the four subtleties of the
        stateful idle machine and the choice of
        \code{EventTimeTimeout} (Section~\ref{sec:impl-idle});
        idempotent sinks and their natural keys
        (Section~\ref{sec:impl-sinks}); and the explicit, never-blended
        Lambda merge (Section~\ref{sec:impl-merge}).
  \item[Nafla M.N.P.] The batch layer trusting nothing and applying no
        watermark (Section~\ref{sec:impl-trust}); delete-then-insert
        recomputation (Section~\ref{sec:recompute}); the poll-not-cron
        DAG schedule; and the alerting design, including the inhibition
        rule (Section~\ref{sec:impl-alerting}).
\end{description}

\section*{Deliberately shared: the \code{common/} package}

\code{common/} is the project's answer to Lambda's central weakness
(Section~\ref{sec:lambda-cost}). Zone mapping, distance, fare,
validation and profitability rules are defined \emph{once} and imported
by the simulator, both processing layers and the API. Each module has a
primary author, listed above, but the rule the team worked to was that
no business rule is defined twice, and a change to a \code{common/}
module is reviewed by whichever members' layers import it. That
constraint is why every member can explain modules they did not
personally write: their own layer depends on them.

\section*{Worked on jointly}

\code{docs/SPEC.md}, written together before any code and treated as
fixed thereafter; \code{docker-compose.yml}, the \code{Makefile} and
\code{common/config.py}, since every member's service is defined in
them; the smoke test, to which each member contributed the checks for
their own layer; the architecture argument of
Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture} and the consistency analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:consistency}; and every acceptance run --- the
fresh-clone start, the multi-day reconciliation and the alert-firing
verification each require all three layers alive at once.

A fuller per-member breakdown, including the test files each member
wrote, is kept in \code{docs/contributions.md} in the repository.
