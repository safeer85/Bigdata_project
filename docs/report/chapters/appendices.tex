Each rule carries a \code{for:} duration of 1--2 real minutes. The
durations are short on purpose: at \code{COMPRESSION=60}, one real
minute is one simulated hour, so a conventional five-minute
\code{for:} would mean waiting five simulated hours before an alert
fired. Every rule has a corresponding entry in
\code{docs/runbook.md} giving what it means, how to reproduce it and how
to fix it.

\section*{Decisions each member defends}

\begin{description}
  \item[Ahamed N.F.F.] Partitioning by \code{vehicle\_id} as an
        ordering requirement (Section~\ref{sec:impl-partitioning}); the
        60$\times$ simulated clock and its derivation from a persisted
        start instant (Section~\ref{sec:simclock}); the deliberate fault
        rates (Section~\ref{sec:impl-faults}); and the decision that
        validation rejects rather than repairs.
  \item[Safeer S.M.] The deliberately boring archiver
        (Section~\ref{sec:impl-archiver}); the four subtleties of the
        stateful idle machine and the choice of
        \code{EventTimeTimeout} (Section~\ref{sec:impl-idle});
        idempotent sinks and their natural keys
        (Section~\ref{sec:impl-sinks}); and the explicit, never-blended
        Lambda merge (Section~\ref{sec:impl-merge}).
  \item[Nafla M.N.P.] The batch layer trusting nothing and applying no
        watermark (Section~\ref{sec:impl-trust}); delete-then-insert
        recomputation (Section~\ref{sec:recompute}); the poll-not-cron
        DAG schedule; and the alerting design, including the inhibition
        rule (Section~\ref{sec:impl-alerting}).
\end{description}

\section*{Deliberately shared: the \code{common/} package}

\code{common/} is the project's answer to Lambda's central weakness
(Section~\ref{sec:lambda-cost}). Zone mapping, distance, fare,
validation and profitability rules are defined \emph{once} and imported
by the simulator, both processing layers and the API. Each module has a
primary author, listed above, but the rule the team worked to was that
no business rule is defined twice, and a change to a \code{common/}
module is reviewed by whichever members' layers import it. That
constraint is why every member can explain modules they did not
personally write: their own layer depends on them.

\section*{Worked on jointly}

\code{docs/SPEC.md}, written together before any code and treated as
fixed thereafter; \code{docker-compose.yml}, the \code{Makefile} and
\code{common/config.py}, since every member's service is defined in
them; the smoke test, to which each member contributed the checks for
their own layer; the architecture argument of
Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture} and the consistency analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:consistency}; and every acceptance run --- the
fresh-clone start, the multi-day reconciliation and the alert-firing
verification each require all three layers alive at once.

A fuller per-member breakdown, including the test files each member
wrote, is kept in \code{docs/contributions.md} in the repository.


\chapter{Defects Found by Operating the System}
\label{app:defects}

Summarised in Section~\ref{sec:defects}. Each was invisible to
code review and visible within minutes of checking the numbers against
expectations.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Defects found by operating the system, and what each
generalises to.}
\label{tab:defects}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{What happened} & \textbf{The general lesson}\\
\midrule
1 & A Spark worker restart killed the archiver's executor. The query
    stayed ``active'', so \code{awaitTermination()} never returned, the
    healthcheck stayed green, and the archiver silently stopped writing
    for \textbf{26 minutes}; the batch layer then reconciled from an
    incomplete archive. &
    \textbf{A healthcheck that proves liveness proves nothing about
    work.} \code{streaming/watchdog.py} now monitors \emph{progress} and
    exits so Docker restarts from the checkpoint --- verified by killing
    executors deliberately.\\
\addlinespace
2 & The first full reconciliation flagged \textbf{35 of 50} vehicles
    unprofitable, with costs at 1.9$\times$ revenue. The pipeline was
    correct; the economics were not calibrated. After recalibration:
    3--6 of 50, 38--48\% margin. &
    \textbf{A correct pipeline can produce a useless answer.} Only
    running it end to end on realistic volumes surfaced this.\\
\addlinespace
3 & The odometer ledger checkpointed every 30 \emph{real} seconds ---
    30 \emph{simulated} minutes at \code{COMPRESSION=60}. A restart lost
    ~90\,km and flagged \textbf{21 of 50} vehicles for a mismatch the
    partner had not caused. &
    \textbf{Intervals chosen in real seconds are 60$\times$ longer in
    business terms.} A check that fires because of our own restart
    trains the reader to ignore it.\\
\addlinespace
4 & Batch gauges defined in \code{common/metrics.py} --- which every
    service imports --- meant all five services exported
    \code{fleet\_batch\_last\_success\_timestamp} at its default of 0,
    and \code{BatchNotRunRecently} fired forever on four bogus series. &
    \textbf{Shared code publishes shared metrics.} A
    permanently-firing false alert is worse than no alert.\\
\addlinespace
5 & The expense SLA was evaluated against every file version, so a
    corrected \code{v2} --- by definition sent after the deadline ---
    marked every resubmission late. &
    \textbf{An alert that fires on a normal operation is noise.} The SLA
    now governs first delivery only.\\
\addlinespace
6 & The ``fresh clone $\rightarrow$ one command'' check exposed two
    Windows-only defects: PowerShell turning Docker's normal stderr
    progress into a terminating error, and a wrapper binding Docker's
    \code{-d} flag to itself. &
    \textbf{Acceptance checks must actually be run.} No amount of
    reading would have found either.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\chapter{At Production Scale}
\label{app:production}

Summarised in Section~\ref{sec:production}. Every row is a
deliberate simplification made for a two-week, single-laptop project,
paired with what running a real fleet would require.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Simplifications made here, and what each becomes at production
scale.}
\label{tab:production}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Today} & \textbf{At scale}\\
\midrule
Ingestion & One broker, RF~1, 6 partitions, 7-day retention;
  \code{schema\_version} plus defensive validation; manual DLQ replay. &
  Three brokers, RF~3, \code{min.insync.replicas=2}. A \textbf{schema
  registry} so incompatible producers are rejected at publish time
  rather than discovered in the DLQ, and an automated replay path.
  Partition count would follow measured throughput, but the
  \emph{keying} would not change --- it is a correctness constraint, not
  a tuning knob.\\
\addlinespace
Storage & Parquet on a Docker volume, partitioned by \code{sim\_date};
  archiver writes every 30\,s. &
  Object storage (a one-variable change --- every path is built from
  \code{LAKE\_ROOT}), plus a \textbf{table format} (Iceberg or Delta) for
  atomic partition overwrites, and \textbf{compaction}, without which the
  batch \emph{read} outgrows the batch \emph{compute}.\\
\addlinespace
Processing & Spark standalone, one worker, 3 cores, both streaming apps
  co-resident; batch in local mode. &
  Kubernetes with the Spark operator. The single most valuable change is
  \textbf{decoupling the archiver from the speed layer} --- they already
  have separate checkpoints but compete for the same cores, which is how
  26 minutes of archiving was lost (defect~1).\\
\addlinespace
Serving & One PostgreSQL instance for both layers, queried directly by
  Grafana. &
  The access patterns diverge: keep PostgreSQL for the \code{rt\_*}
  point lookups, add read replicas so dashboards cannot slow the
  streaming sinks, and move \code{batch\_vehicle\_daily} to a columnar
  warehouse once history outgrows one node. Add caching on
  \code{/fleet/live} and authentication.\\
\addlinespace
Observability & Prometheus, Alertmanager, Grafana, structured JSON logs,
  correlation IDs. &
  \textbf{Log aggregation} (Loki or ELK) --- the logs are already
  structured JSON with a \code{stage} field precisely so this is a
  deployment step, not a code change. Then \textbf{real tracing}, once
  multiple producers make ``which producer caused this DLQ spike?''
  unanswerable from correlation IDs. Then \textbf{SLOs with error
  budgets}~\cite{beyer2016sre}: \code{IdleVehiclesHigh > 8} is tuned to a
  50-vehicle fleet and does not survive it doubling.\\
\addlinespace
Clock & Simulated time derived from wall-clock elapsed time, so downtime
  does not pause the world. &
  \textbf{Annotate known outage windows}, so downstream consumers --- and
  the \code{becoming\_unprofitable} trend rule in particular --- can
  exclude degraded days rather than mistaking an incident for a
  commercial decline.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
